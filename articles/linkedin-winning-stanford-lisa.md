# Winning Stanford GSB LISA at 14: What I Learned About Building

Most people think startups begin with companies.

They usually begin with questions.

For me, the question was: **why does modern health feel so complicated?**

Thousands of supplements. Endless advice. Contradictions everywhere. Meanwhile, ancient systems like Ayurveda and Traditional Chinese Medicine hold centuries of knowledge, but it's buried and scattered across sources no one can trust.

That question became **rooted.ai** — and it's what I brought to the Stanford GSB LISA accelerator.

---

## The Pitch

Pitching an early idea is strange. You're describing something that doesn't fully exist yet. The pitch centered on three things:

1. **The problem:** Health advice is fragmented and contradictory
2. **The opportunity:** AI can organize massive bodies of traditional knowledge
3. **The vision:** A system where anyone can explore natural remedies, their history, and their ties to modern science — making health clearer, not noisier

LISA isn't just about pitching. It's about thinking like a founder. It forces hard questions: *Who is this for? Why now? What makes it unique? Why you?*

Those questions create clarity. And clarity is a founder's most valuable asset.

## What Surprised Me

A surprising lesson: **age mattered far less than curiosity.**

People cared about ideas, not credentials. If you're genuinely trying to build something meaningful, people listen. A 13-year-old with a clear vision gets the same attention as a seasoned entrepreneur — if the idea is real.

## The Real Value

Winning LISA was exciting, but the real value was refinement. The program forced me to:

- Sharpen the problem definition
- Clarify the target audience
- Strengthen the technical vision
- Practice communicating complex ideas simply

That's what early startup experiences offer: not just recognition, but *sharpening*. You come out the other side with a clearer understanding of what you're building and why.

## What I'm Building Now

Since LISA, I've shipped multiple products:

- **Loopy** — an autonomous software engineer that builds from a single idea
- **Supercompress** — neural context compression for AI agents (cuts token costs by ~65%)
- **ascii-skill** — a zero-dependency ASCII art engine for AI agents
- **Pincer** — a Chrome extension for dyslexic and neurodivergent users
- **Jasmine** — AI frontend design with real taste

All of this started with a question about health. That's the thing about building — you never know where a good question will take you.
